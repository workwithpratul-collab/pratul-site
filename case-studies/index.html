The stories behind the work.

01Dermatology clinic

DERMAÉ

Concept

The people

Think of someone booking their first appointment for a skin worry they've lived with for years. They're a little nervous, and they've seen too many clinics promise miracles. Before anything else, they want to feel safe.

What the site had to do

Feel like walking into a calm, careful clinic, and make asking for a consultation feel easy, not like a sales pitch.

What I built

A short film you drive with your scroll: a drop of serum on skin, then a slow drift into a quiet treatment room, past the treatments, the doctor and the care journey. When it ends, the page turns plain and practical: treatments you can filter by what's bothering you, answers to 25 common questions, and booking in three short steps.

Key decisions

  1. Honest words, by rule.

    Health websites slide easily into promises. Every treatment says what it's designed to do, never "guaranteed" or "permanent", and every sample review, figure and before-and-after is labelled as a sample.

  2. A film you never have to sit through.

    One button on the first screen glides past the film to the treatments, and every section ends with a clear next step. Someone who already knows what they want shouldn't have to wait.

  3. Start from the worry, not the medical term.

    Visitors pick what's bothering them and the matching treatments open. "Book for this concern" carries that choice straight into the booking form.

Concept DERMAÉ isn't a real clinic. I made it to show clinics what their website could be, so there are no patients or results to report.

02Online shop

Plant nursery

Client project

The people

Buying a plant online takes a little faith. The buyer is picturing a box arriving with snapped stems and yellow leaves, and wondering whether the plant in the photo will look anything like the one at their door.

What the site had to do

Show the care before the sale, so ordering a plant feels as safe as walking through the glasshouse. And then make buying easy, the way the nursery's customers already like to buy.

What I built

This one shows I can build 3D, interactive e-commerce, not just a pretty page:

  • A cinematic film of the nursery's work, played by scrolling: the greenhouse at golden hour, a monstera misted and wrapped by hand, the delivery van, and a room filling with plants.
  • 360° plants you can turn, every plant and pot, by dragging, swiping or using the keys.
  • Product pages with care guides, details and "Choose a pot" with a live total.
  • Styling projects for homes, cafés and offices, with before and after sliders.
  • Ordering online or on WhatsApp: a cart that goes through a checkout, or straight to the nursery as a message.

Key decisions

  1. Show the care, not just the catalogue.

    The film follows a day of real work, so buyers see how their plant is looked after before it ever reaches them. The nursery's name only appears at the end, when its gold crest glides into the menu.

  2. Let people turn the plant round.

    A photo hides the back of a plant. In 360° you can drag it round, like standing at the bench, and see exactly what you're getting.

  3. Order the way people already do.

    Plenty of customers would rather send a message than fill in a form, so every cart can go to the nursery on WhatsApp as well as through a regular checkout.

Client project Built for Akash's nursery. It isn't live yet, so there's no link to share. Until it launches, the prices, photos and reviews on it are placeholders.

03Two sites

Advertising & branding agency

Pitch demo

The people

When a business hires an agency, it's handing over its name. Before signing, the owner wants to see how a job really gets done: who prints it, how it's checked, and whether it will look right on a street corner.

What the site had to do

Show the work itself, not just talk about it, and make the first conversation easy for clients who aren't sure how to describe what they need.

What I built

I pitched Trinetra Creations, a printing and branding agency in Raipur, two ways to tell their story. The first is one page with a film you drive with your scroll: a blank sheet is printed, carried out, installed and lit up as a giant hoarding over the city.

The second is a full agency site: eight pages with case studies, services, a free consultation, a journal, and an order builder that turns "I need a banner" into a clear brief, step by step.

Key decisions

  1. Follow one job from start to finish.

    Instead of a gallery of finished signs, the film follows a single hoarding from the printer to the street, so a client can see the care in every step.

  2. Make the first step small.

    Many clients don't know the words for what they need. The free consultation and the order builder ask simple questions, then put the answers together into a brief.

  3. Made for a phone held upright.

    The film has its own set of frames drawn for tall phone screens, so it fills the screen instead of shrinking into a letterbox.

Pitch demo Both sites were made to pitch the agency. The projects, clients and numbers on them are samples.

04Mediterranean restaurant

Olive & Ember

Concept

The people

Choosing where to have dinner is often a family decision made on a phone, half an hour before leaving. Will it feel special? Is there something for everyone? Can we get a table tonight?

What the site had to do

Let the warmth of the room come through the screen, and turn that feeling into a booking.

What I built

A warm, editorial site in ivory, olive and terracotta: big serif headlines, the food and the room in large photographs, a menu to linger over, a gallery, and a reservation form that checks itself as you type. "Reserve a Table" is waiting in the header and on the first screen.

Key decisions

  1. Let the food set the mood.

    Large, warm photographs and an editorial layout make it feel like a magazine about the place, not a list of dishes.

  2. Keep the table one tap away.

    "Reserve a Table" sits in the header and on the first screen, so the moment someone feels hungry, the next step is right there.

  3. Restraint over decoration.

    Few colours, lots of space and gentle motion. A good restaurant doesn't shout, and neither does its website.

Concept A restaurant I imagined, to show what a warm, booking-first site can do. It isn't live yet.

What ties them together

Four very different businesses, one way of working. Each started with a person on the other side of the screen and one feeling the site had to give them: safety, trust, proof, warmth. The design, the motion and the code are all there to serve that feeling.
