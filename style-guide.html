06 Rules of the look

Eight rules every future step follows.

The same list lives in CLAUDE.md, so every working session starts from it.

  1. Paint, don’t render.Surfaces are soft gradients of chosen colours. No photo textures, no visible facets, no shiny highlights, no hard edges between light and shade.
  2. No black, no hard shadows.The darkest value in a scene is that scene’s deep colour (navy at night, lilac at sunrise). Shade is a cooler colour, never a darker grey.
  3. Warm means light.Saturated amber, orange and gold come only from light sources: tents, lanterns, the sun, and the summit flag (the “Book a call” moment). Everything else stays soft and cool.
  4. Distance is paler.Every layer further away leans towards the horizon colour, so the world reads as stacked painted layers with mist gathering low between them.
  5. One palette, one place.World colours come only from palette.js, interface colours only from tokens.css. Never type a colour straight into a scene file.
  6. The light moves as one.Sky, fog, mist, key light, trees and the trekker change together through setClimb(). Never animate one of them on its own.
  7. Calm motion only.Slow, eased camera and interface moves (ease out, 300 ms or more). Nothing bounces, flashes, shakes or swooshes.
  8. Small figure, big world.The trekker is a small warm note in a large quiet landscape. Leave open sky, and keep text as real HTML in the calm parts of the frame.

07 Decisions

What we chose, and why.

Body font

Considered Inter (the Phase 1 placeholder), Instrument Sans, Figtree, DM Sans and Manrope.

Chose Manrope: open, slightly rounded shapes feel warm next to the sharp serif, it stays very readable at 14 to 16 px over a busy 3D background, and its 600 weight gives buttons presence without shouting. Inter is cold and seen everywhere; Instrument Sans is too narrow for long reading; Figtree is a little too playful for a premium tone.

Headline and labels

Kept Instrument Serif (it already gives the film its title voice) and IBM Plex Mono (the field notes voice for labels and the altitude). Labels moved from 11 and 13 px to one 12 px step, so every size sits on one scale.

Interface colours

Considered pure white text with a dark panel behind it, and dark text on light panels for the bright scenes. Both fight the film.

Chose moonlit ivory text (#F5F0E8) with a soft indigo shadow, and a single lantern amber accent (#FFB877). Ivory never glares at night, and the shadow keeps it readable over the peach and gold skies (see the four stages above). Amber is the only warm UI colour, so it always means “this is the action”, the same way warm means light in the world.

Buttons

Considered square buttons, outlined only buttons, and filled pills.

Chose pills: soft shapes match the painted world. The primary is filled amber and on hover “turns up the lantern” (lighter amber, warm halo, the arrow drifts 4 px). The secondary is a quiet glass pill with an ivory hairline, so it never competes with the primary.

Altitude meter

Considered a horizontal progress bar at the top, a percentage counter, and a vertical rail.

Chose a thin vertical rail in the bottom left with the camp name and “ALT 3,450 m”: climbing reads as going up, the four camps become ticks, and it lines up with the headline’s left edge. Altitudes: base camp 3,450 m, ridge 3,980 m, meadow 4,410 m, summit 5,120 m.

Cursor

Considered a large blend mode blob and a trailing ring. Blobs hide what you point at.

Chose a 6 px dot that never lags (aiming always feels exact) plus a thin ring that follows softly and opens over links. Off on touch screens, and the ring stops moving with reduced motion.

The meadow

Considered a close, flower filled foreground (like the reference photos) and a wide valley view.

Chose a wide, layered view: rolling rows of grass that turn lilac with distance, the forested valley in mist, peaks catching the first sun on the right, and the trekker small on a pale trail. Flowers are small pastel drifts, so warm colour stays with the light. The sun’s light is aimed a little higher than the visible sun, so the grass gets pale gold light instead of a flat graze.

The summit

Considered a close summit ridge with a big flag, and a view down the far side with the sun behind us.

Chose looking straight into the sunrise over a sea of clouds: gold where the clouds face the sun, cool lilac in their hollows, snow in shade turning lilac too. The flag is a small warm note on the crest beside the trekker: the “Book a call” moment, framed by the brightest part of the sky.
